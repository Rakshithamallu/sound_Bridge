When Should You Get a Hearing Test?

Most adults prioritize annual dental cleanings, routine eye exams, and blood pressure screenings, yet regular audiological assessments are often neglected until noticeable communication breakdowns occur. Understanding when and how frequently to evaluate your hearing can mean the difference between effortless communication and permanent auditory pathway deterioration.

Why Regular Hearing Screenings Are Vital

Because sensorineural hearing loss develops gradually over years, you cannot rely purely on self-perception to gauge auditory decline. An initial diagnostic hearing test in Bengaluru establishes your personal auditory baseline, allowing audiologists to track subtle threshold shifts over time before they impact your social and professional life.

Recommended Screening Milestones by Age

The American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA) and leading audiological bodies recommend clear milestones for routine hearing health evaluations:

Age Demographic Recommended Frequency Primary Focus
Infants & Newborns At birth (OAE / BERA) + 6 months Congenital hearing detection & speech foundation
Children (3–18 yrs) Every 2 years / Prior to schooling Otitis media detection & classroom comprehension
Adults (18–49 yrs) Every 3 to 5 years (baseline) Recreational noise damage & acoustic health
Seniors (50+ yrs) Annually Presbycusis, speech discrimination & cognitive protection

Sudden Hearing Loss: An Audiological Emergency

While most hearing loss is gradual, Sudden Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SSHL) occurs abruptly—often noticed as an unexplained blockage or deafness in one ear upon waking up. This is a medical emergency requiring immediate consultation with an ENT specialist and audiologist within 48 to 72 hours for corticosteroids and diagnostic evaluation.

Bengaluru Urban Noise & Headphone Hazards

Living in Bengaluru presents distinctive acoustic challenges. From persistent traffic congestion on Outer Ring Road and metro construction to hours spent wearing in-ear wireless earbuds during IT conference calls, noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL) is rapidly rising among professionals in their 20s and 30s. If you listen to personal audio devices above 60% volume for more than 60 minutes daily, annual audiological screening is highly advised.

Chronic Health Conditions & Auditory Risk

Your inner ear requires intense microvascular blood flow to function. Systemic health conditions directly impact cochlear microcirculation:

  • Type 2 Diabetes: Adults with diabetes are twice as likely to develop sensorineural hearing loss due to microvascular nerve damage.
  • Cardiovascular Disease & Hypertension: Blood flow irregularities can permanently impair the oxygenation of the stria vascularis in the inner ear.
  • Ototoxic Medications: Certain chemotherapy drugs, aminoglycoside antibiotics, and heavy NSAID usage require strict audiometric monitoring.

What to Expect During Your Evaluation

A clinical hearing evaluation at SoundBridge is thorough, non-invasive, and comfortable. Conducted inside a dual-walled soundproof chamber calibrated to ANSI/ISO acoustic standards, the assessment includes otoscopic video examination, Pure Tone Audiometry (air and bone conduction), and Speech Discrimination Testing. You receive a complete printed audiogram and comprehensive counselling from our licensed clinical team.
