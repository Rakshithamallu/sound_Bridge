When Should You Get a Hearing Test?

1. Recommended Hearing Screening Frequency by Age

Routine audiometric assessment establishes your personal baseline audiogram, making subtle yearly threshold changes detectable long before severe communication impairment sets in.

Age Group / Profile Recommended Frequency Primary Clinical Rationale
Newborns & Infants At birth (OAE / BERA) Early identification of congenital loss critical for speech development.
School-Age Children (3–12) Every 1–2 years / School entry Undetected middle-ear otitis media or mild sensorineural loss affects learning.
Adults (18–49, No Noise Exposure) Every 3–5 years Baseline threshold tracking and preventive counselling.
Industrial / High-Noise Workers Annually (Annual Occupational) Monitoring Noise-Induced Hearing Loss (NIHL) threshold shifts at 4 kHz.
Adults Aged 50+ Every 1–2 years Presbycusis (age-related decline) tracking to prevent auditory deprivation.

2. Key Symptoms Demanding an Immediate Evaluation

You should not wait for your scheduled milestone if you observe any of the following clinical symptoms:

  • Persistent unilateral or bilateral tinnitus (ringing, whistling, buzzing, humming).
  • Feeling of fullness or pressure inside the ear canal that does not clear with swallowing.
  • Difficulty understanding speech in restaurants, places of worship, or during phone calls.
  • A history of taking ototoxic medications (certain chemotherapy agents, aminoglycosides, high-dose loop diuretics).
  • Head trauma, whiplash injury, or sudden acoustic blast exposure (fireworks, industrial machinery).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a hearing test painful or uncomfortable?

Not at all. Pure Tone Audiometry and Tympanometry are completely non-invasive, gentle, and comfortable procedures performed in a soundproof room.

How long does a comprehensive hearing evaluation take?

A complete diagnostic evaluation—including video otoscopy, pure-tone air and bone conduction testing, and speech audiometry—typically takes 30 to 45 minutes.
