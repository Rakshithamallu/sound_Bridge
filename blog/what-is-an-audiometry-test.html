What Is an Audiometry Test? Step-by-Step Clinical Guide

1. The 4 Stages of a Comprehensive Hearing Evaluation

  1. Video Otoscopy: The audiologist visually inspects your external ear canal and eardrum with a high-definition video camera to ensure there is no wax blockage, perforation, or inflammation.
  2. Pure Tone Audiometry (Air Conduction): Seated in a double-walled ANSI-calibrated soundproof booth wearing supra-aural or insert earphones, you press a response button whenever you hear faint tones across 250 Hz to 8000 Hz.
  3. Bone Conduction Audiometry: A small vibrating oscillator is positioned on the mastoid bone behind your ear, bypassing the outer/middle ear to measure true sensorineural inner-ear reserve.
  4. Speech Audiometry & Tympanometry: Evaluates your Speech Reception Threshold (SRT) and Word Discrimination Score (WDS), accompanied by tympanometry to assess middle-ear eardrum mobility and pressure.

2. How to Read Your Resulting Audiogram

Your audiogram is a graph with pitch (frequency in Hertz) on the horizontal axis and volume (intensity in decibels) on the vertical axis. Hearing thresholds between 0 to 20 dB HL represent normal adult hearing; thresholds lower on the chart indicate mild, moderate, severe, or profound loss.
